Most organizations don't have a "data problem" so much as a **data management problem**: information exists, but it's scattered across systems, inconsistently formatted, and hard to trust. Here are five practices that consistently make the biggest difference.

## 1. Integrate before you analyze

Pulling data from five different systems into five different spreadsheets isn't integration — it's just relocation. Before running any analysis, get source systems feeding into a single, consistent data layer (a warehouse or equivalent). This is the single highest-leverage step in any data management program.

## 2. Treat data quality as an ongoing process, not a one-time cleanup

A one-time data cleanup degrades the moment new, unvalidated data enters the system. Quality assurance needs to run continuously — validation rules, deduplication, and consistency checks applied as data arrives, not just when someone notices a problem.

## 3. Put governance controls in place before you need them

Access controls, encryption, and clear ownership of each dataset are far easier to design upfront than to retrofit after a compliance question or security incident forces the issue.

## 4. Design for the data you'll have, not just the data you have today

Scalable data management architecture accounts for new sources and data volume growth. Rebuilding a data pipeline every time a new system is added is a sign the original design didn't account for growth.

## 5. Make data accessible to the people who need it — securely

Data management isn't just about storage and security; it's also about making the right data available to the right people without a two-week ticket process. Role-based access strikes that balance.
